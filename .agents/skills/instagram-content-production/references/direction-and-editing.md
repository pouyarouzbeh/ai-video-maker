# Direction and editing

## Before production

- Use one viewer question or outcome and define the sequence: hook within three seconds for a reel, build-up, payoff, ending. Write what the viewer sees first and what they should understand by the end.
- For substantial projects, choose two or three reference frames in the brief. For each, record subject, composition, negative space, what attracts the eye first, and how contrast supports reading. Pick a limited palette and at most two type families. Define brand choices per project. Check mixed-direction text and code as separate readable blocks.
- Mark each reference by its purpose. For generated assets, record the prompt, model when known, anchor frame, and what must match: identity or silhouette, material, environment, light and camera direction. Select and correct an anchor frame before generating related views. Save meaningful manual corrections in the manifest or shot notes.
- For important shots, record opening and closing frames, shot size and angle, motion and reason, focus and intended reading hold. Choose real footage to show a real action, diagrams for relationships, and 3D for depth, occlusion, physical scale or a point of view that explains something.

## Edit the animatic

- Build a low-resolution full cut on scratch narration. After recording or synthesis, replace timing estimates with measured phrase windows. Leave time to predict and time to read the result. Give each important cut a reason and record where the eye should land.
- Prefer clear action and visual continuity across cuts. Use music beats for selected accents. Place Foley at the contact or event and use continuous ambience across cuts within the same space. Keep speech distinct and preserve intentional pauses.
- Use editable keyframes and easing for important title entrances and exits. Keep the middle hold legible and restrict motion blur to the fast portion. Test captions on a phone, with the sound muted, using current interface overlays as a visual check.

## Finish and review

- Match exposure, white balance and color temperature across three representative frames. Then apply the creative look. Record Blender view transform, delivery color space and any LUT applied. Check the actual composite and exported clip for duplicate transforms, clipped detail or mismatched shots.
- Judge lighting in a contact sheet, also in grayscale when hierarchy is unclear. Check contact shadows, surface textures and consistency of atmosphere. Add subtle camera motion and grain only where they work on a phone and retain readable detail.
- Have QA watch the full preview at normal speed with and without audio. Record item or timecode, observed issue and one proposed fix. Example: add eight frames of hold before the answer. Review the revision, then verify the exported media separately.
- For explainers, compare picture, narration, code and captions on the same example. Where practical, ask three target viewers to predict a new case and explain their reasoning. For the first pilot use two successful explanations out of three as the working criterion. Use the moment of confusion to guide the revision.
